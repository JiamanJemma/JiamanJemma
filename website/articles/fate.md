# Fate Is Not Written in the Stars: It Is Walked into Existence

## Unlocking the algorithm of destiny through Liaofan's Four Lessons, Quantum Mechanics, and the ancient art of Mindful Speech.

Recently, I revisited a classic text of Eastern wisdom, Liaofan's Four Lessons (《了凡四训》). It is not merely a book about morality; it is a manual on how to hack the seemingly rigid code of destiny.

Many of us ask: "Is my life predestined?"

If I were to summarize my findings in a single sentence, it would be this: **Fate is not a script written by Heaven; it is a path forged, step by step, by your every thought and choice.**

Here, I want to share three pillars of wisdom—breaking the illusion of determinism, the weight of speech, and the power of intention—to help you reclaim the pen and write your own story.

---

## I. The Collapse of Possibility: Why We Believe in "Fate"

In Liaofan's Four Lessons, the protagonist, Yuan Liaofan, had his entire life predicted by a fortune teller named Master Kong. Every exam rank, every government position, even the length of his life—everything was calculated with terrifying precision.

Believing this calculation, Liaofan's life paused. He stopped striving. He stopped hoping. He simply waited for the script to play out.

This reminds me of Quantum Collapse in modern physics. A quantum state holds infinite possibilities, but the moment it is "observed," it collapses into a single, fixed state.

**Fate works the same way.** The moment you believe "this is my lot in life," your infinite potential collapses into a single, narrow reality. The tragedy isn't that fate exists; the tragedy is the death of the heart that accepts it.

Ancient Chinese philosopher Mozi (墨子) criticized this fatalism over two thousand years ago. He said:

> "Fatalism is the fabrication of tyrants and the excuse of the destitute; it is not the language of the benevolent." (Mozi, "Anti-Fatalism")

Fatalism is a mental anesthetic. To accept it is to surrender your agency.

Zen Buddhism offers the ultimate counter-strike to this rigid thinking:

> "No fixed abode, lively and active." (Records of the Ancient Worthies)

If your mind is fluid and unattached, your fate remains elastic. If your mind is rigid, your fate is dead. Destiny is merely the shadow cast by your mind upon the world.

---

## II. The Weight of Words: Speech as Energy

If the mind is the architect, then speech is the tool. How do we start changing our fate? By guarding our speech.

There is a chilling line in the ancient text Random Words for Children (《小儿语》):

> "In the ruin of a family or the destruction of a person, speech accounts for eight parts out of ten."

Eighty percent of a person's misfortune stems from their mouth. It is not a lack of ability that destroys people, but the chaotic energy of their words.

Liaofan's Four Lessons and other classics offer a protocol for "Energy Management through Speech":

1. **Adapt to the Times (Confucius)** — Confucius taught us to read the room—or rather, the era. *"When the state is governed by reason, speak and act boldly. When the state is in darkness, act boldly but speak with humility."* This is not hypocrisy; it is wisdom. When the wind is fair, sail; when the wind is foul, lower the mast.

2. **Directness Without Harm (Zhuangzi)** — The Taoist philosopher Zhuangzi described the ideal communication: *"Straightforward without causing injury."* (虽直而不病) To be honest without being toxic. To be sharp without cutting others. This requires a mind free of "illness"—free of malice and ego.

3. **Silence as Protection (Chen Lu)** — *"Few words to avoid slander; few desires to preserve the self."* Silence is not emptiness; it is a choice. It is the conservation of energy.

---

## III. The Source Code: The Power of Intention

Why is it so hard to control our speech? Because speech is just the symptom. The mind is the root.

Liaofan realized a profound truth:

> "Seeking is within me. I can seek not only moral virtue but also merit and wealth. This seeking is beneficial to obtaining."

Fate is not something you wait for; it is something you "seek" (求)—not by begging the gods, but by cultivating the self.

**This is the algorithm of reality:**

1. **The Mind (Intention)**: If your desires are unchecked, your mind becomes chaotic. As Chen Lu said, "Few desires to preserve the self." A calm mind is the source code for a stable life.
2. **The Speech (Action)**: When the mind is clear, speech becomes "straightforward without injury." You stop leaking energy through complaints, gossip, and arrogance.
3. **The Fate (Result)**: When your energy is conserved and directed, the "quantum collapse" works in your favor.

Feng Dao (冯道), a politician who survived four dynasties, wrote:

> "Do not despair when the times are dangerous; the future always holds causes yet to unfold."

Even if the present looks bleak, do not let your heart collapse. The seeds you plant today—in your mind and with your words—are the causes of tomorrow's destiny.

---

## Conclusion

Fate is a trend, but the heart is the rudder.

The script is not finished. It is being written right now, by the thought you are holding and the words you are about to speak.

**Fate is not written in the stars. It is walked into existence.**
